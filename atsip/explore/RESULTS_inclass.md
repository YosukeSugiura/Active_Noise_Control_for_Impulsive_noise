## Notes on the run

- W0 has no mu in RESULTS_specfac.md. Its mu (0.2) is the specfac rule (six-source white whole mean, no divergence) over the specfac grid, from `out/inclass_w0_select.json`.
- Stability uses a 17-point grid from 0.01 to 2 (geomspace), not the 12 points from 0.05 to 2, because the predicted maximum at rho >= 20 dB lies below 0.05 times the rho=0 value. Section 3b is a stricter criterion added after the task rule showed no effect of rho.
- Section 2 (final-weight distances) is confounded. B's nominal tail (25-30 s) makes B and A share their last five seconds, so B vs A is closer than the cross-seed baseline by construction. C vs A pairs share the input realisation, so they are closer than cross-seed pairs too. Neither comparison isolates a shift of the mean fixed point; time-averaged weights and more seeds would be needed.
- Section 1 L1 and L2 use the windows of the task: whole (all samples), first5 (first 5 s), last10 (last 10% of samples, i.e. last 3 s).
- Section 5 uses per-block NM because the 64-block grid is coarser than SF's transient (SF is below -15 dB at its first sample).
- Gain sequence g_i depends on the reference R only (Srr is an EMA of |R|^2), so E[g^2]/E[g] is identical for every method on a given input.

## Conclusion

1. (i) holds approximately: the existing causal Wiener solution moves by about -32 dB (C vs A, 10 s solver) and -35 to -37 dB (30 s solver, B and C vs A), relative to w_opt(A). B's 10 s window is identical to A's by construction, and no seed-to-seed baseline was computed for w_opt, so this is not a test at the noise level.
2. (ii) is not decided: final-weight distances B vs A (-50 to -69 dB) and C vs A (-32 to -42 dB) are not separable from the cross-seed baseline (-30 to -44 dB), and the B pairs are confounded by the shared nominal tail (see Notes).
3. (iii) fails in the stated form: under the task rule the max stable mu is the same at every rho (1.44 or 1.03 on the grid, ratio 1.000, against predicted 0.145 at 10 dB and 0.05 to 0.06 at 20-40 dB); under the stricter whole-ANR rule only B0 drops, to 0.72 at 20 dB and above, and W0.5 and SF do not move.
4. (iv) is partly supported: for SF the spread of crossing times across A, D1.45 and D1.2 is smaller in cumulative g than in block index at all four thresholds (e.g. 1.38 against 1.67 at -10 dB, 1.04 against 1.43 at -20 dB); for W0.5 it is smaller at -10 dB (1.18 against 1.67) but larger at -20 dB (1.16 against 1.07). The excess-MSD claim fails in sign: across the seven inputs the final NM falls (gets better) as E[g^2]/E[g] grows (Spearman -0.96 for W0.5, -0.79 for B0, -0.39 for SF, n = 7).
5. L1 and L2 agree that W0 is worst on every input and that SF+ramp is top on L2 for six of seven inputs (GS top on C by 0.10 dB), but they differ on the ranking at the top and on C: L1 puts GS first on D1.2 (by 0.23 dB over SF+ramp) where L2 puts SF+ramp first (by 0.35 dB); on C, L1 ranks W0.5 ahead of B0 by 1.3 dB where L2 ranks B0 ahead by 0.07 dB.
